2a) Item 7 (right-angle used to pass one end to opposite side of heel)
Reframe item intent: “Suture end is safely transferred to the opposite side of the heel without twisting/trauma.”

Score “Yes” if ANY of these are true:

A right-angle is used (classic method), OR

An alternative instrument is used (forceps/needle driver) to pass the suture end around/through the heel region safely, AND:

No visible tissue tearing, snagging, or lumen intrusion

No suture crossing/twisting that compromises heel alignment
Score “No” only if:

Transfer is not performed (surgeon continues on same side incorrectly), OR

Transfer causes malalignment, twisting, or clear tissue injury/unsafe passage
Score “Unable” if the transfer moment is fully off-screen.

Important: In evidence, explicitly state whether it was “Right-angle used” vs “Equivalent safe transfer observed.”

2b) Item 9 (toe trimmed to appropriate size at apex)
Retrospective raters often check “Yes” if the toe is already appropriately shaped and no trimming is needed, even if “trims” is written.

Rewrite scoring definition:
Score “Yes” if EITHER:

Trimming is observed and results in an appropriate toe/apex fit, OR

No trimming is observed BUT the toe/apex fit is appropriate throughout closure (no redundancy/dog-ear, no tension-shortage, no mismatch at apex)
Score “No” only if:

Toe/apex fit is clearly inappropriate (redundancy, bunching, or shortage/tension) and is not corrected
Score “Unable” if the apex/toe region is not visible enough to judge fit.

This change alone will remove a lot of false “No” calls compared to human retrospective checklists.
