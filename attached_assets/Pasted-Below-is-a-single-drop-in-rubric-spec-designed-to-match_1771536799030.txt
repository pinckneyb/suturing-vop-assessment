Below is a single, “drop-in” rubric spec designed to match how your surgeons are actually using the retrospective checklist: they will fail for a small set of red-line problems (e.g., anchoring at the toe) , but they will still pass performances that are “not pretty” if the technical steps/outcome are acceptable , and they do not treat some named method steps (e.g., right-angle pass) as strictly required . It also bakes in the spacing insight: surgeons comment both “too close”  and “ok to space bites further apart” , so the rubric must score spacing functionally (uniformity + approximation) rather than “mm metrology.”

VOP Retrospective-Aligned Rubric Spec (drop-in) v4

1. Scope and intent
   1.1 Purpose: Generate outputs that are apples-to-apples with surgeon retrospective VOP checklists, while producing stable, auditable AI results.

1.2 Key alignment principles derived from surgeon patterns
A. Proficiency is driven by red-line failures (sequence/anchoring and clear leak/approximation risks), not perfect stylistic form. Toe anchoring can produce a “Not Proficient” even when other elements are decent .
B. “Method” items (e.g., right-angle pass) are not always gating; surgeons may mark the method “No” yet still rate overall proficient .
C. Spacing is not literal millimeters; surgeons coach both directions (“too close” and “space further apart”)  . Score spacing by uniformity + approximation/no gaping.
D. Common coaching themes should be captured as structured “tags” (e.g., arteriotomy short ; spatulation geometry convex vs concave ; avoid grabbing suture with forceps/pickups ; don’t tie directly on the toe ; needle direction reversal ; use of assistant ).

2. Two-stage architecture (recommended, optional but strongly stabilizing)
   Stage 1: Factual Observation Record (FOR)

* Produced by a video model (Gemini or other).
* Must be purely descriptive: actions, sequence, visibility. No “good/bad,” no scores, no “proficient.”

Stage 2: Scoring Engine

* Applies this rubric deterministically wherever possible.
* Uses a text model only for Item 14 narrative and for resolving explicit “unknown” fields (not re-watching the video).

3. Global scoring policies (configurable)
   3.1 Visibility policy

* Non-observable aspects default to YES (your current policy), but MUST be flagged as ASSUMED with a short reason.

3.2 Spacing policy (critical)

* “Spacing” items (back wall and front wall) are scored by: consistency + approximation + gaping/leak-risk, not by millimeters.
* Hard ban: the system must not output numeric “mm” spacing claims unless a validated measurement proxy exists (default: never output mm).

3.3 Technique equivalence policy

* Items phrased as a specific instrument or method are scored by intent/outcome and may be satisfied by equivalent technique, unless a red-line failure is triggered.

4. Checklist items 1–14: operational definitions

Items 1–4 (setup steps; often off-screen)

1. Oblique incision 2x graft lumen with #11

* YES if observed, or ASSUMED YES if off-screen.

2. Avoids back wall injury

* YES unless there is clear evidence of posterior wall catch/through-and-through injury.

3. Spatulates graft (Metzenbaum on form)

* YES if spatulation is visible at any point, or ASSUMED YES if off-screen and graft appears spatulated.

4. Correct suture (5-0 Prolene/Surgipro)

* YES if packaging/metadata confirms, else ASSUMED YES under your policy.

Items 5–7 (heel strategy and progression; equivalence enabled)
5. “Places double-ended suture at heel”
Score by heel establishment, not by double-armed literalism.

* YES if the heel is correctly established (aligned graft/artery edges) and remains stable, regardless of single- vs double-armed start.
* NO only if the initial anchoring is at the toe (or otherwise incorrect for heel strategy) and causes difficulty coming around the heel (red-line) , or if heel alignment is clearly wrong/unstable.

6. “Ties knot outside at heel”

* YES if knot is outside/extraluminal and appears seated.

7. “Uses right angle to pass suture end to opposite side of heel”
   Score by safe progression to the opposite side (or method not requiring a pass).

* YES if either (a) right-angle used, or (b) equivalent safe progression occurs without twist/trauma, or (c) single-armed continuous technique makes “pass the other end” irrelevant and the back wall is completed without twist.
* NO only if lack of transfer/progression causes twist/malalignment or an inability to complete properly.
  Note: surgeons can mark this “No” and still pass overall , so Item 7 is non-gating unless it creates downstream technical failure.

Items 8–11 (product/outcome; most predictive)
8. Back wall completion (“~1 mm apart” on form)
Functional spacing rule:

* YES if back wall is completed with reasonably uniform spacing and good edge approximation, with no visible gaping/dog-ears/leak-risk.
* NO if spacing/needle placement causes visible gaping, edge mismatch, or major irregularity consistent with leak risk (this is one of the patterns associated with a fail when paired with other issues, like bite mismatch/unsafe handling) .

9. Toe trimming at apex
   Score by apex fit, not by whether trimming is seen.

* YES if the toe/apex fit appears appropriate (no bunching/dog-ear/redundancy or tension shortage), whether or not trimming is observed.
* NO only if apex fit is clearly inappropriate and not corrected.

10. Front wall completion (“~1 mm apart” on form)
    Same functional spacing rule as Item 8.

* YES if uniform enough + well-approximated + no gaping.
* NO if visible gaps/edge mismatch or gross irregularity consistent with leak/stenosis risk.

11. Final knot secure and outside

* YES if knot is outside and appears seated/secure.

Item 12: Economy of Time and Motion (1–5)
Economy is not “speed.” Do not penalize careful slowness unless it produces wasted motion (repeated resets, searching, frequent re-grasps).
Require the scorer to output:

* flow_organization: organized / mixed / disorganized
* wasted_motion_events: up to 5 discrete events with brief descriptions

Recommended mapping guardrails:

* 4–5: organized + 0–1 wasted-motion events
* 3: mixed OR 2–3 wasted-motion events
* 1–2: disorganized OR 4–5 wasted-motion events

Item 13: Final Rating / Demonstrates Proficiency (derived in code)
Compute in the app; do not let the model “vote.”
Proficiency = NO if any red-line is present:
Red-line A: Toe anchoring strategy that creates difficulty coming around heel (explicit fail pattern) 
Red-line B: Outcome risk: visible gaping/malapposition on back or front wall (Items 8 or 10 = NO)
Red-line C: Clear unsafe handling that threatens integrity + correlates with poor bites/spacing (e.g., grabbing the suture line with pickups + uneven bites), especially when it creates malapposition 
Red-line D: Intraluminal knot or clearly insecure knot (Items 6 or 11 = NO)

Otherwise Proficiency = YES, even if:

* Item 7 method is “No” but outcome is fine 
* The work is “not pretty” but steps/outcome are achieved 

Item 14: Other Summative Comments
Output two parts:
A. One-sentence outcome summary (what matters clinically: approximation, heel/toe, knots).
B. 1–3 coaching tags (see below) rendered as plain language.

5. Coaching tags (structured, non-gating unless they trigger a red-line)
   These tags are for regularization and feedback; they should not flip proficiency unless they directly produce malapposition/unsafe outcome.

Recommended taxonomy (examples drawn from surgeon comments):

* ARTERIOTOMY_TOO_SHORT 
* SPATULATION_CONCAVE_GEOMETRY (recommend convex) 
* SUTURE_GRASPED_WITH_FORCEPS_OR_PICKUPS (risk fray/integrity) 
* TIE_DIRECTLY_ON_TOE (recommend just beyond apex) 
* NEEDLE_DIRECTION_REVERSED (out-to-in / in-to-out mismatch) 
* ASSISTANT_QUALITY_IMPACT (benefitted from good assistant / needs better assistant use) 
* SPACING_TOO_CLOSE (surgeons do comment this) 
* SPACING_CAN_BE_WIDER (surgeons also comment this)
