Right now your “Economy of Time and Motion” score is behaving like a different instrument than the surgeon’s. You’ve solved most checklist alignment by changing scoring rules; economy needs its own calibration, and you should treat it as a separate model-output problem.

Three practical fixes that work in this exact situation:

Anchor the model to the surgeon’s 1/3/5 examples, but add a “speed vs waste” separation
Your AI is likely penalizing slowness (pauses, careful re-grasps) as “wasted motion,” while at least one surgeon (818) appears to reward careful, organized flow even if it isn’t fast.

Update the prompt so economy is scored on two subcomponents, then mapped to 1–5:

Motion waste (reaching, searching, unnecessary repositioning, redundant instrument swaps)

Flow organization (clear plan, consistent hand positioning, no loss of orientation)
Then: “Do not penalize slow pace unless it causes extra wasted motion or repeated resets.”

Map to 1–5 with a rule like:

5 = low waste + high organization

4 = low waste + mostly organized (minor waste)

3 = moderate waste OR moderate organization

2 = frequent waste OR disorganized flow

1 = frequent waste + disorganized flow

This stops “slow but clean” from automatically becoming 2/5.

Force evidence-based scoring with a fixed count of “wasted-motion events”
Make the AI justify economy with a small tally. Example:

“List up to 5 wasted-motion events you observed. If you cannot list at least:

1 event → economy must be 4–5

2–3 events → economy must be 3

4–5 events → economy must be 1–2

Do not reuse the same event description twice.”

This usually prevents over-penalizing with vague language (“frequent micro-adjustments”) and makes the score auditable.

Calibrate by regression to your surgeon set (simple and effective)
You already have paired surgeon/AI economy scores for multiple cases. Use them to fit a simple correction:

Collect (AI score, surgeon score) for 30–50 videos.

Fit a linear mapping or isotonic calibration (monotonic).

Apply that mapping to future AI economy scores.

This is low-effort and tends to eliminate systematic bias quickly. It also reveals whether the issue is global (always too low/high) or conditional (e.g., only for certain camera views, needle drivers, or task segments).
